# Evidence for proposal round two

## From a person who knows the quiz's audience

> "I can guarantee that the average joe will not know what turrets are when
> they are mentioned in the context of clouds."

The perceive experts agree in substance: for "Did little turrets rise from
its top?", when a cloud really has turrets only about 6 in 10 people say yes,
and 2 or 3 in 10 say yes when it has none. Measured noise 0.25; the knowledge
base had guessed 0.10.

## What round two measured

Every question now has error rates fitted from how untrained people would
answer it. Much less reliable than first guessed: `hooks` (noise 0.30),
`shading` (0.29), `element_size` (0.27), `turrets` (0.25), `base` (0.20),
`mamma` (0.20), `halo` (0.17). More reliable: `colour` (0.02), `sky_cover`
(0.07), `top` (0.07), `depth` (0.08).

With perfect answers the quiz names 30 of 32 clouds. Cirrus castellanus and
Stratocumulus castellanus are the two it misses: each is told from its
relatives only by `turrets`, and dropping `turrets` would leave
Stratocumulus castellanus answering every question exactly as Stratocumulus
stratiformis does. The pair Cirrus fibratus / Cirrus uncinus rests on
`hooks` alone in the same way.

## What round one's proposals did

Round one proposed 15 questions. Three were merged (`sun_view`, `cloud_form`,
`falling_visible`) and one question dropped (`anvil`). With measured rates, no
other proposal gained 2 points: `veil_texture` looked like a gain on a few
games and lost on many. Questions whose options most people mix up, or that
give nearly every cloud the same answer, do not pay for the question they
cost. Round one's questions and their measured error rates are in the pool
