Who the ten are: ordinary adults who have never read about the subject.
They know everyday words (cloud, sky, rain, grey, fluffy, big) and nothing
more. They have never learnt what words mean in this field, and they do not
look anything up.

How to answer:
- First read the question and each option as they would, word by word. Any
  word used in a special sense for this subject, or any everyday word used
  in a way they would have to have explained to them (the way a sailor says
  "veer" or a doctor says "lesion"), is one they do not understand. Count
  everyone who would need it explained as unable to answer, with the reason
  unclear_question, unless the rest of the sentence makes its meaning
  plain.
- For each case, say how many of the ten would pick each option, and how
  many could not answer at all. The counts add up to ten.
- Count people as they are: some misread the question, some mix up options
  that sound alike, some pick the first one that roughly fits, some are sure
  and wrong. Do not count what an expert would say, and do not credit them
  with knowing what you know.
- If anyone could not answer, give the main reason:
  not_observable: they could not see or know it from where they stand;
  ambiguous: they could not choose between the options;
  unclear_question: they would not understand the question or its options;
  unknown: none of these.
  With nobody unable to answer, give no reason.
- Answer each case on its own, by its number.
